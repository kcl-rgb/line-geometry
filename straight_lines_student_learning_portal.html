The Three Fundamental Relationships for Two Straight Lines

Let two straight lines in standard Cartesian form be: $L_1: A_1x + B_1y + C_1 = 0$ and $L_2: A_2x + B_2y + C_2 = 0$.

1

Perpendicular Lines ($L_1 \perp L_2$)

Angle $\theta = 90^\circ$

$A_1A_2 = -B_1B_2$ or $A_1A_2 + B_1B_2 = 0$

The product of their slopes is $-1$: $$m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1$$ Converting to Cartesian form avoids division by zero for vertical lines!

✓ Intersects at an exact $90^\circ$ angle
2

Parallel Lines ($L_1 \parallel L_2$)

Example 4.18(b) • No Intersections

$$\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{B_1}{B_2} \neq \frac{C_1}{C_2}$$

The lines have identical slopes ($m_1 = m_2$) but different $y$-intercepts. Hence they never meet in the plane.

✓ Exactly 0 points of intersection
3

Coincident Lines

Example 4.18(c) • Identical Lines

$$\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{B_1}{B_2} = \frac{C_1}{C_2}$$

One equation is simply a non-zero multiple of the other. Every single point on $L_1$ also lies on $L_2$.

✓ Infinitely many points of intersection

📖 Step-by-Step Proof of the Perpendicular Condition ($A_1A_2 = -B_1B_2$)

Here is the complete derivation using only elementary Cartesian coordinate algebra (no vectors):

Step 1: Convert to Slope-Intercept Form ($y = mx + c$) For line $L_1$: $$A_1x + B_1y + C_1 = 0 \implies B_1y = -A_1x - C_1 \implies y = \left(-\frac{A_1}{B_1}\right)x - \frac{C_1}{B_1}$$ Thus, the slope of $L_1$ is: $$m_1 = -\frac{A_1}{B_1}$$ Similarly for line $L_2$: $$A_2x + B_2y + C_2 = 0 \implies m_2 = -\frac{A_2}{B_2}$$
Step 2: Apply the Perpendicular Slope Theorem Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular if and only if their slopes multiply to $-1$: $$m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1$$ Substitute our slope expressions: $$\left(-\frac{A_1}{B_1}\right) \cdot \left(-\frac{A_2}{B_2}\right) = -1$$ Since $(-1) \cdot (-1) = 1$: $$\frac{A_1A_2}{B_1B_2} = -1$$
Step 3: Multiply Both Sides by $B_1B_2$ $$A_1A_2 = -B_1B_2 \iff A_1A_2 + B_1B_2 = 0$$ Q.E.D. — Notice that $A_1A_2 + B_1B_2 = 0$ remains completely valid even if one line is vertical ($B_1=0$) and the other is horizontal ($A_2=0$)!
